Let's say you have calculations you need to perform in your data that are too slow to query live at scale. You'd like to cache the data, but how do you keep it updated on demand in the most efficient way? Trigger-based solutions can work, but are complex and maintenance-intensive. There are platform-specific features in SQL Server, for example, such as change tracking and change data capture. Many databases support materialized views, but they have costs, trade-offs, complications, and assumptions of their own. What if you wanted a more generic approach that leverages native EF Core functionality? That's what MaterializR for.

MaterializR supports two kind of cache table via abstract classes:
- [SetCache](MaterializR.Core/SetCache.cs) manages calculations based on sets of rows along one or more _dimensions_ with one or more _facts_. An example would be total sales by region and year.
- [RowCache](MaterializR.Core/RowCache.cs) manages calculations derived from a single row. An example would be the total price of line items on a sales order.
